Method

How a rating is made.

No black box. The sequence, the arithmetic and the gates are below, and every one of them is applied in the code you are running.

It is not a research report, not investment advice, and not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold. Nothing here is a solicitation or a forecast of returns. 1. Data, then arithmetic

Five years of daily bars and the current quote come from Yahoo Finance. Indian fundamentals — market cap, P/E, book value, dividend yield, ROE, ROCE — come from the company page on Screener. Headlines come from the Bing News RSS feed, which is keyless and dates every item. All of it is fetched through a proxy because browsers block cross-origin reads; the proxy only forwards, it stores nothing.

Each figure is tagged with its source and the time it was fetched. A field that could not be fetched is shown as [N/A — not found]. Nothing is filled in from memory.

2. Scenarios carry the rating

Three scenarios — bull, base, bear — each with a growth rate, an exit multiple and a probability. Base growth is the five-year EPS CAGR, faded toward 10% across the forecast rather than projected flat, so a cyclical peak is not treated as permanent. The exit multiple is today's, capped. Each scenario gives a 12-month price; the probability-weighted value produces an expected return, and that decides the rating:

RatingCondition
Strong buyExpected return ≥ +30%, confidence ≥ 8, reward:risk ≥ 2:1
BuyExpected return ≥ the hurdle (15% by default), confidence ≥ 6, reward:risk ≥ 2:1
HoldBetween −10% and the hurdle
Sell≤ −10%
Strong sell≤ −25% with confidence ≥ 8

3. Headlines can only take risk off

Company and national headlines are scored by keyword: positive, negative, severe and announcement-type. Tone adjusts confidence by a fraction of a point. A severe story — fraud, probe, resignation, downgrade — costs a full point, blocks a fresh buy and raises the hurdle by five points. A hostile macro backdrop raises it by two. Headlines never create a buy. This reads titles, not articles, and every headline is printed so you can disagree.

4. Confidence is four scores

Data quality, thesis clarity, catalyst visibility and valuation cushion, averaged, adjusted slightly for headline tone, and capped at 6 whenever a key input is missing. A thin-data report cannot masquerade as a high-conviction one.

5. The trade plan is bounded by your limits

The stop comes from swing support and ATR, not from a round number. Position size is the smaller of your per-position cap and the size where a stop-out costs your per-trade loss limit. If reward:risk is under 2:1 the report says so and prints the price at which it would qualify.

6. What is deliberately missing

Segment mix, moat evidence, governance history, analyst targets, shareholding trend and concall commentary need paid or filing-level access. Those sections render with the specific gap named, plus the free sources worth checking. Headlines are shown but not read in full. You can paste fundamentals in by hand — the engine recomputes immediately.

7. Publishing it yourself

This is a static site. Drag the folder onto Netlify Drop, or push it to a GitHub repository and enable Pages, and it is live on a free host with no build step. The optional proxy worker is free too. See README.md.
